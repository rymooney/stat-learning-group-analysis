For the data exploration, we wanted to examine the differences in professional sanction issued between hallucination items: fabricated, false quotes, and misrepresented.

The Professional.Sanction variable describes if a professional sanction, official penalties or disciplinary measures imposed on a licensed worker for an ethical violation, was given.

This gives the proportion of cases receiving a professional sanction among cases with and without a false quote, a fabricated, and a misrepresented hallucinations. We can see that the proportion of those issued a professional sanction is quite small across the hallucination items.

I am curious if having a misrepresented hallucination in the case correlates with an increase in a professional sanction being issued. Since Professional Sanction is a binary response variable, we can perform logistic regression.

analysis_data <- analysis_data |>
  mutate(
    sanction = if_else(`Professional.Sanction` == "Yes", 1, 0)
  )

model <- glm(
  sanction ~ misrepresented,
  data = analysis_data,
  family = binomial
)

The logistic regression estimated that cases with a misrepresented hallucination had 1.74 times the odds of receiving a professional sanction compared with cases without one (\(\beta_1\) = 0.5532, p = 0.000746). A logistic regression model is appropriate because the response variable, Professional.Sanction, is binary, and logistic regression is designed to model the probability of a binary outcome based on the predictor variables. A limitation is that the model only includes whether a case contained a misrepresented hallucination and does not account for other factors such as the severity or number of hallucinations, the type of proceeding, or other potentially significant explanatory variables.
